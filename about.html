The Greenwood Tavern

Some places are built.
Some are returned to.

We sit on the New Jersey shore of Greenwood Lake — on a site with a history older than the state itself, on a lake that has long memory, and next to water that notices how you behave.

Est. 1847  ·  Closed 1866  ·  Returned 2025

The Greenwood Tavern opened on October 1, 2025, on the same New Jersey shore where the original Greenwood Tavern stood in 1847. Same lake. Same site. Different century. Same idea: good food, honest prices, everyone treated the same regardless of who they are. Nothing wasted. The lake honored. The land respected.

We're a lakeside bar and restaurant in Hewitt, NJ. We serve brunch on weekends, lunch and dinner every day, and we host live music, community events, and the occasional trivia night. We believe in this community and we believe in this lake.

We also believe the lake has watched everything that ever happened on its shore, and has opinions about it. That's not marketing. That's just what you start to think when you spend enough time out here on a foggy night.

What follows is the story we know. The parts we can verify, we have. The rest… well. The lake keeps its own records.

The History
A Long Story, Briefly Told

In 1847, a man named Appleby Awosting opened a tavern on the New Jersey shore of what was then called Long Pond Lake. He named it for the dense woods surrounding the property — The Greenwood Tavern — and locals took to the name so readily that they began calling the lake itself "the lake by the Greenwood." That name stuck. Awosting's didn't.

The Tavern became the social center of a growing community. It was also, by most honest accounts, run by a man who saw the community primarily as something to profit from. He had partners of similar character. For nearly two decades, the Tavern operated as the kind of establishment that serves everyone a drink while quietly picking their pockets with the other hand.

The lake, apparently, noticed.

There are old stories about Greenwood Lake — older than Awosting, older than the ironworks, older than the first European to lay eyes on it. Stories about something that lives in the deep water off the southern shore. Something with wings folded against a scaled body and eyes that glow faintly green in the fog. Something patient, territorial, and not particularly forgiving of people who disrespect the water or the community that depends on it. The Ramapough Lenape, who knew this land long before anyone else, had a name for it: Quampique.

On a spring night in 1866, Awosting closed up for the evening. The fog had been building off the southern shore since sundown. He counted his money — every coin, every bill — stacked it on the bar, stepped outside, and was never seen again. The following morning, locals found the Tavern unlocked, chairs stacked, bar wiped clean, and every coin and bill exactly where he'd left it. Appleby Awosting would sooner have left his own hand behind than that money.

The Greenwood Tavern closed that week. The site passed through various hands over the following 159 years. The story faded from local memory into folklore into something people half-remember from a grandparent's warning about the fog.

On October 1, 2025, someone decided to open a bar and restaurant on that same shore. They named it The Greenwood Tavern. They intended to do it right: good food, honest prices, everyone treated the same regardless of who they are. Nothing wasted. The lake honored. The land respected.

Somewhere in the deep water off the southern shore, something old and patient stirred slightly in its sleep.

And settled back down. For now.

How We Run It
What We Actually Believe
🌿

Honor the Lake

We're on the shore because of this lake. We take that seriously. We try to run a clean operation, give back to the community that surrounds the water, and treat the land with the respect it's earned over twelve thousand years of history.

🍺

No Hierarchy at the Bar

Everyone gets the same service regardless of who they are. That's not a policy. That's the whole point. The original Greenwood Tavern failed this test. We intend to pass it.

🎵

Community First

Live music, trivia nights, school fundraisers, family events — we're here because this community exists. The lake doesn't just deserve a good bar. It deserves a place that acts like a good neighbor.
